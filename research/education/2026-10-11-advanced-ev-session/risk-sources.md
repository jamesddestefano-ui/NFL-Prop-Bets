## Supporting primary papers

### Risk constraint versus robust criterion

- Busseti, Ryu and Boyd (2016), [Risk-Constrained Kelly Gambling](https://stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/kelly.pdf), with [author page](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/kelly.html). Relevant definitions and §§2–5 reviewed in the accessible PDF. The constraint concerns falling below a fraction of initial wealth under the paper's model assumptions, including IID returns and the stipulated distribution. It is not a universal peak-to-trough drawdown guarantee and was not applied to NFL data here.
- Sun and Boyd (December 8, 2018), [Distributional Robust Kelly Gambling](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/robust_kelly.pdf), with [author page](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/robust_kelly.html). Introduction, finite-outcome formulation and uncertainty-set definition reviewed from local PDF text after a web fetch timed out. Worst-case growth over a chosen set differs from posterior averaging. No uncertainty set was empirically justified for NFL props.
- Browne and Whitt (1996), [Portfolio Choice and the Bayesian Kelly Criterion](https://doi.org/10.2307/1428168). **Abstract/bibliographic review only.** The publisher abstract describes a posterior-mean-based optimal policy under its random-environment model. An author PDF was unreadable in web text extraction; direct download returned 403. No full-paper review or universal multi-period extension is claimed.

### Check the experiment's operational definitions

- Haghani and Dewey, [Rational Decision-Making Under Uncertainty: Observed Betting Patterns on a Biased Coin](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.01427). Working draft October 19, 2016; arXiv January 4, 2017. Complete eight-page paper reviewed.
- **EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE, non-NFL experiment:** 61 participants, 30-minute sessions and a $250 cap. Page 3 footnote 7 defines “bust” as ending below $2 and “maxing out” as at least $200. The reported 28% and 21% should not be relabeled as exactly $0 and exactly $250. The paper treats Kelly as a useful heuristic, not necessarily the optimum for its capped, finite-time experiment with outside wealth.
- This limited experiment and its behavioral observations do not measure NFL forecasting, the user's betting performance, or the suitability of any personal allocation.

## Independence and claim boundary

These sources introduce a portfolio-risk perspective. The Stanford papers share an author group; MIT and the podcast are additional teaching/practitioner sources, not independent replications of NFL edge. The mathematical examples in the supplement are original, assumption-driven constructions. They are supported by payoff identities and independent calculations, not by claims that an educator's own strategy is profitable.
