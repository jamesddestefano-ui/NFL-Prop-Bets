- Title: **Steamers and Drifters Revisited: Evidence for Closing Market Efficiency?**
- URL: https://football-data.co.uk/blog/steamers_drifters_revisited.php
- Publication: September 20, 2018, displayed article date.
- Retrieval: 2026-10-11 approximately 19:55 UTC.
- Access: complete article text reviewed; video timestamps not applicable.
- Scope: 158,092 soccer matches, August 2007–July 2017; 474,276 outcome-price pairs. Selections shortening more than 10% returned −3.0% at Pinnacle closing prices; reported steamer/drifter closing-return difference p = 0.12.

### T5 — Proper scoring rules

### A1 — Comparing de-vig methods

Stephen Clarke, Stephanie Kovalchik and Martin Ingram (2017), **Adjusting Bookmaker’s Odds to Allow for Overround**, American Journal of Sports Science 5(6):45–49. DOI: 10.11648/j.ajss.20170506.12. Publisher date: December 25, 2017.

https://www.sciencepg.com/article/10.11648/j.ajss.20170506.12

Retrieved 2026-10-11 approximately 20:06 UTC. Publisher abstract and citation metadata reviewed; the full paper was not newly reviewed for this register. The abstract compares additive, normalization, Shin and power adjustments across three sporting datasets and reports better power-method performance than normalization in those datasets. Support: de-vig choice involves assumptions and can matter empirically. The abstract's comparative claim is confined to its studied datasets; it is not validation of a universally superior method for NFL props.

### A2 — Model-selection bias

Gavin C. Cawley and Nicola L. C. Talbot (2010), **On Over-fitting in Model Selection and Subsequent Selection Bias in Performance Evaluation**, Journal of Machine Learning Research 11:2079–2107. PDF publication month: July 2010.

https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html

https://jmlr.org/papers/volume11/cawley10a/cawley10a.pdf

Retrieved 2026-10-11 approximately 20:06 UTC. Publisher abstract and PDF introduction/evaluation discussion inspected; not a new complete-paper review. Support: tuning a finite-sample selection criterion can overfit it and bias subsequent evaluation; selection belongs within the evaluated modeling procedure. NFL chronological holdouts and clustering are application-specific design choices, not experimental findings from this paper.
