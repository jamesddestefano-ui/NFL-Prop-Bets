## Mathematical interpretation and worked counterexample

VERIFIED MATHEMATICAL PRINCIPLE: an expected count E[N] does not alone identify P(N ≥ 1). Invented next-game TD distributions:
- A: P(N=1)=0.5 and P(N=0)=0.5.
- B: P(N=2)=0.25 and P(N=0)=0.75.
Both have expected count 0.5; anytime-TD probabilities are 50% and 25%.

At purely hypothetical +150 (decimal 2.5), break-even is 40%. A gives +25% expected ROI; B gives −37.5%. Those are counterexample distributions, not fitted player models or conservative bounds. No-vig probability is unavailable without an opposing same-contract quote. Fair decimal odds are 2 and 4, respectively; probability edges are +10 and −15 percentage points. No real player fair probability, uncertainty interval, playable line/juice or Kelly stake is justified. Historical three-week xTD is further removed from this next-game probability than even E[N] in this example.

MODEL ASSUMPTION: using P(TD ≥ 1)=1−exp(−lambda) would impose Poisson structure and require a forward-looking lambda. Dividing historical xTD by three does not validate that structure, future role or opponent conditions. Current v2 touchdown gates still require calibrated probabilities, verified contract/price, central ROI at least 15%, and nonnegative conservative EV.

## Testable improvement for PRIME review

UNTESTED HYPOTHESIS: recent opportunity sequence improves next-game TD probability beyond a market-only baseline more than aggregate expected-minus-actual TD deficit does.

Predeclare one eligible contract universe and timestamp cutoff. Preserve all screened candidates, missing data and abstentions. On development weeks specify features and shrinkage, then freeze:
A. same-contract no-vig market baseline;
B. baseline plus aggregate three-week deficit;
C. baseline plus recent opportunity sequence and verified current role context.
Evaluate paired Brier/log-loss and calibration on subsequent chronological weeks; group uncertainty by game/week. Preserve predictions before outcomes and quote changes. Compare nested feature additions to avoid attributing all of C's effect to recency. No threshold tuning on held-out outcomes.

Failure conditions: no held-out improvement, unstable signs across weeks, advantage erased by detection-time prices, or dependence on unverified model versions. Insufficient coverage yields INCONCLUSIVE, not success. No empirical test was run here.

METHODOLOGY_CHALLENGE / MODEL_UNCERTAINTY: reject an automatic “TD debt” boost. Counterargument: historical opportunity could proxy persistent goal-line role, and regression toward a well-specified conditional model may be useful. Resolve with role evidence and held-out tests, not by assuming either deficit or trend is automatically predictive.
